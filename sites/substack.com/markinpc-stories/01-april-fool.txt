I pulled this on Janie—my ex. Perhaps, in part, it's one of the reasons she's my ex.

The Florida lottery does not play a big part in my life, but every now and then, when the prize is a multi-million dollar one, I'll buy a ticket. The drawing is on Saturday evening, and I knew Jane would love nothing better than being a multi-millionaire. In fact, she'd asked me to buy her a ticket. She was up in Georgia on April First, at a horse show and wouldn't be back until Sunday night.

Perfect—the stage was set. I bought the lottery ticket for her and left the newspaper out on her chair with the winning numbers.

A few hours after she came home, she was relaxing in her easy chair and saw the newspaper. She asked me about the ticket, which I gave her dutifully, and an instant later I heard the shriek.

"Oh my God! We won! We won!"

"For real?" I asked.

"Yes! Oh my God, for real! I checked it twice—we won!"

"We are multimillionaires!" I came out to look and the numbers matched. I yelped. "I don't believe this."

Jane was already on the phone, calling one of her friends.

"Don't say anything," I cautioned. "We need to talk to a lawyer."

"Why do I need to do that?"

"Well, there could be problems."

"Like what?" she asked.

"Well," I said, "it could be that I bought a ticket this morning with last week's numbers. The ticket is worthless."

There was silence.
